[Lesson 5](/learn/product/mvp-weekly-scope) gave you a thin Now list and a week. This lesson is how you **lead** so that cut survives: the job of the leader, how an empowered team turns the ship, what changes as you move from IC to staff to group PM, and how you work a room when nobody reports to you.

You do not need a title to do this job. You need a scoreboard, a vision people can repeat, and the courage to own the outcome.

## 1. Job of the product leader

Product leadership is not a louder backlog. <Key>You own the outcome and the conditions that let the team hit it.</Key> You do not own every decision. Horowitz's [Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager](https://a16z.com/good-product-manager-bad-product-manager/) still names the split: the job is a product customers love on terms the company can measure. Tasks are Tuesday. Leadership is whether the scoreboard moves and whether the team still trusts you when it does not.

Five duties. Miss one and the others rot.

### Clear uncertainty

The cone from [Lesson 1](/learn/product/how-pm-begins) stays wide until someone forces a diagnosis. Your job: name what is unknown, kill fake certainty, and pick the next cheap test ([Lesson 4](/learn/product/experiments)). A leader who "keeps options open" forever is avoiding the job.

**Netflix / Qwikster:** Hastings owned the split of DVD and streaming as a public bet. When subscribers left, the company reversed. The leadership move was not the original plan. It was clearing the fog after the scoreboard spoke.

### Take blame

When the bet fails, you stand up first. You do not hide behind "the team disagreed," "sales promised it," or "engineering was slow." Bad leaders distribute blame and hoard credit. Good leaders reverse that.

### Distribute rewards

Credit eng, design, research, and GTM in the rooms that matter: exec sync, launch notes, all-hands. Rewards follow contribution to the outcome, not proximity to the slide deck. If you take the stage alone after a win, the team learns to stop bringing you their best work.

### Lay down and communicate vision

Vision here is [Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy) made speakable: where we play, how we win, what we refuse. Say it until a skeptic can repeat it. A roadmap without a vision is a shopping list. A vision without repetition is a memo nobody read.

### Empower and motivate with purpose

Purpose is the user job plus the company scoreboard, not a poster. People need the *why* and the authority to act inside constraints. Motivation without empowerment is pep talk. Empowerment without purpose is abandonment.

| Duty | Good leader | Bad leader |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty | Names the unknown; runs a test | Pretends the plan is settled |
| Blame | Owns the miss in public | Points at the team or "the market" |
| Rewards | Credits the people who moved the metric | Takes the spotlight |
| Vision | Repeats refusals and beachhead until sticky | Ships a new slogan each quarter |
| Purpose | Ties the week to a user job and a scoreboard | "Ship faster" with no why |

## 2. Empowered team: turn the ship

Marty Cagan's [*Inspired*](https://www.svpg.com/books/) draws the line: **feature teams** wait for a prioritized backlog and build what they are told. **Empowered product teams** own problems and outcomes. They discover and deliver against a scoreboard.

L. David Marquet's [*Turn the Ship Around!*](https://davidmarquet.com/turn-the-ship-around-book/) is the operating system for that empowerment. Marquet took command of a nuclear submarine crew trained to wait for orders. Leader-follower kept the boat safe only when the captain was omniscient. He pushed control to where the information lived: officers stated intent ("I intend to…"), he checked clarity and competence, then let them run. Authority moved down. Thinking moved up.

Product work is the same trap. A PM who approves every solution is a captain on every watch. The team stops thinking. Delivery slows to the PM's calendar.

### Leader-leader, not leader-follower

| Mode | What you hear | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Leader-follower | "What should we build?" / "Is this approved?" | Feature factory; PM as bottleneck |
| Leader-leader | "I intend to run twelve beachhead interviews with this stop rule" | Team owns discovery and delivery inside your intent |

Your job as product leader: set **destination, constraints, appetite, and what must not break**. The team's job: solutions, weekly slices, kill decisions on weak ideas. You do not grip every tiller.

Marquet's sequence before you push control:

1. **Clarity:** vision, beachhead, refusals, scoreboard the team can repeat ([Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy))
2. **Competence:** they know how to run a hypothesis card, cut Now, and read the metric ([Lessons 4–5](/learn/product/experiments))
3. **Then control:** they decide the how without asking permission for each tiller twitch

Push control without clarity and you get chaos. Push control without competence and you get accidents. Hold control when both exist and you get a queue.

**Amazon** working backwards is clarity before control: the press release and FAQ force the outcome into language customers would believe. The team invents the path. **Feature-factory orgs** reverse it: tickets first, outcome never named. That is leader-follower with a Jira skin.

Bad "empowerment" is Marquet's failure mode in a hoodie: vague goal, no beachhead, no refusals, no appetite, then blame the team for chaos. Real empowerment is clear intent + people skilled enough to act + authority to act.

| Move | Leader owns | Team owns |
|---|---|---|
| Intent / clarity | Outcome, beachhead, refusals, appetite | Repeat it back; challenge gaps |
| Competence | Coaching on cards, Now cuts, scoreboard literacy | Run interviews, prototypes, synthesis |
| Control | Air cover for Later; no mid-week strategy rewrite | Slice, ship, instrument; "I intend to…" on the week |
| Scoreboard | What success means this quarter | Honest events and callouts |

The experiment review tests whether the team can use that control. For instance, a team tests a shorter onboarding flow:

- The primary metric, first-value completion, rises from 40% to 47%.
- The diagnostic metric shows fewer customers abandoning the setup form.
- The support-contact guardrail rises above the limit set before launch.

A leader does not approve the rollout because one number is green or reject the work because another is red. The leader asks whether the team used the agreed segment and counting rule, whether the primary result cleared its threshold, and what caused the guardrail failure. The team can keep the learning, repair the missing explanation, and test again without asking the leader to design the flow.

[Experiments](/learn/product/experiments) defines primary, diagnostic, and guardrail metrics. Leadership makes the team state them before exposure and defend the complete result afterward.

When the scoreboard moves, the team turns the ship without a new captain's order for every degree of heading. When the scoreboard stalls, the leader clears uncertainty again: new card, new stop rule, not a louder standup. That is still leadership. It is not a return to leader-follower for every ticket.

## 3. IC, staff, and group PM

The five duties stay the same. **Scope** changes.

| Scope | What you lead | What "good" looks like | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IC PM** | One product area; one Now board | Thin Now; clear card; team can say the vision back | Hero PM who absorbs every decision |
| **Staff PM** | A problem space; coaches IC PMs; owns hard seams (platform, GTM, compliance) | ICs get sharper; seams have owners; fewer cross-team collisions | Still writing every PRD; no coaching |
| **Group / Head of** | Portfolio of teams; shared strategy and bet mix | Shared outcome across teams; staffing matches the refusals | Roadmap aggregation with no shared scoreboard |

**Staff PM:** you spend less time as the sole author of discovery and more time as the person who makes other PMs dangerous in a good way: sharper hypotheses, cleaner Now lists, courage to kill pets. You take the seams nobody wants: the API contract between two teams, the compliance gate, the GTM promise that would break the spine.

**Group PM:** you defend [Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy) at portfolio scale. You decide which teams exist for which outcomes. You stop "twelve Now boards that sum to everything." Melissa Perri's [*Escaping the Build Trap*](https://www.melissaperri.com/escaping-the-build-trap) failure mode at this altitude is output theater: every team busy, no company metric moved.

**Case:** a group PM inherits five teams, each with a feature roadmap. The fix is not a mega-Gantt. It is one shared outcome, a refusal list, and three Now boards that serve that outcome. Teams that cannot name their link to the outcome get a learning week or a merge.

Titles vary by company. Judge yourself by **scope of alignment and coaching**, not by the word on LinkedIn.

## 4. Influence without authority

Most PMs lead people who do not report to them. Map the room before you talk.

Three axes:

1. **Power:** who can fund, block, or reassign
2. **Interest:** who cares about this bet this month
3. **Information:** who holds customer, tech, or market truth you lack

Work high-power / high-interest people first with evidence and a clear ask. High-power / low-interest people get short updates and no surprise escalations. Low-power / high-interest people often hold the information; bring them into discovery.

**Slack** spread inside companies because one team could adopt without a procurement cycle. The "stakeholder" was often a team lead who felt the pain, not the CIO. Influence followed the job, not the org chart.

Do not confuse a RACI spreadsheet with leadership. The map is a tool for this week's ask. Update it when the bet changes.

## 5. Forums that work

Pick the room for the decision. Kill status theater.

| Forum | Decision it owns | What does not belong |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy / quarterly | Beachhead, refusals, scoreboard | Ticket grooming |
| Planning (week / sprint) | This week's learn or build slice | Reopening strategy |
| Review / demo | Did the slice move the metric; what to cut | Feature wishlist from the audience |
| Exec sync | Tradeoffs that need air cover or funding | Live redesign of the UI |

If every meeting re-litigates strategy, you have no forums; you have a standing argument. Put strategy in its room. Put the week in its room. Defend the boundary.

## 6. Escalate vs absorb

Escalate **risk and tradeoffs**: missing metric, safety, legal, a refusal under attack, a bet that needs more appetite.

Absorb **noise and preference**: color of the button, "my cousin hated the copy," a stakeholder's pet feature with no card.

Good PM escalates early with options and a recommendation. Bad PM escalates late as a crisis, or escalates every preference to avoid owning a call.

**Script shape:** "We believed X. Evidence says Y. Options are A (cheap), B (more appetite). I recommend A because ___. I need your cover on Later item Z staying out."

## 7. Defend Now / Next / Later

[Lesson 5](/learn/product/mvp-weekly-scope) taught how to fill the board. Leadership is how you **protect** it.

Bring to the room:

- The live hypothesis and stop rule
- What entered Now and why
- Named Later items (refusals) so sales cannot resurrect them quietly
- The metric you will read at the end of the week

Do not re-open [Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy) every Tuesday. If someone wants Stripe, certificates, or a second site before the demand signal, point at Later and the card that would have to pass first.

Staff and group PMs defend a **shared** board language across teams. Same columns. Same meaning of Now. Different rows.

## 8. Leading eng, design, and GTM

Same scoreboard. Different craft. You are not the proxy engineer or the proxy designer.

Echo [Lesson 4](/learn/product/experiments): PM owns the belief and the success rule. Design owns the experience the human reacts to. Eng owns the smallest path that makes the metric real. GTM owns the promise to the market, and must not invent a promise the product refused.

When crafts collide, return to intent: outcome, beachhead, appetite. The fight about the solution is welcome. The fight that erases the scoreboard is not.

## 9. Specimen: this site

No fake VP org chart. Leadership duties still apply on a solo or founder-PM build.

| Duty | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| Clear uncertainty | Waitlist before the tech curriculum; interviews before twelve eng lessons |
| Take blame | Weak lesson copy or a confused home page is on the author, not "the stack" |
| Distribute rewards | Cite books and practitioners; credit community feedback in the open |
| Vision | Tech-for-PMs beachhead; free PM as trust; packaging only if demand earns it ([Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy)) |
| Purpose | Readers who need stack literacy for reviews and timelines, not vanity traffic |

Stakeholders without an org: public readers, LinkedIn / PM communities, waitlist signups, future packaging buyers, and the personal scoreboard (teach + showcase even if demand is thin). When someone asks for login, Stripe, or certificates before the waitlist earns them, that ask goes to Later: same move as in a company room.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What is happening |
|---|---|
| Leadership = more meetings | Forums without decisions |
| Empowerment = no constraints | Abandonment dressed as trust |
| Staff PM still writes every PRD | Scope did not change; burnout did |
| Group PM = concatenated roadmaps | No shared outcome |
| Take credit, distribute blame | Team stops bringing truth |
| Re-open strategy every week | No Now to defend |
| Escalate preferences | You are not doing the job |

## Before you go on

1. Write the **five duties** for one live bet in one sentence each.
2. Name whether you are acting as **IC, staff, or group** this month, and one behavior that would prove the scope.
3. Draw a small **stakeholder map** (power × interest) for that bet. Circle your first three conversations.
4. Write a four-line **escalation script** for a Later item someone will try to pull into Now.
5. If you lead a team: write **clarity** for the next week (outcome, constraints, appetite, scoreboard). Ask each person for one **"I intend to…"** for the week. Do not hand them a solution list.
