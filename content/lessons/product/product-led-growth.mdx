[Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy) named the motion: product-led. This lesson is how that motion works, when it fails, and how it differs from sales-led growth. [Lesson 6](/learn/product/product-leadership) taught you to protect the bet in the room. Here you protect the **growth model** from accidental complexity: ads theater, SDR theater, or a paywall before anyone activated.

## What PLG is

<Key>Product-led growth means the product is the primary way people discover, try, adopt, and expand.</Key> The demo is the product. The sales call, if it exists, closes what the product already proved.

Wes Bush's [*Product Led Growth*](https://www.productled.com/product-led-growth-book) is the field guide. OpenView popularized the term. The PM job is not the buzzword. It is designing a path where a beachhead user reaches value without a human in the loop, then measuring whether they come back.

**Sales-led** growth puts humans first. A **sales development representative (SDR)** finds and qualifies prospects; an **account executive (AE)** runs the deal through demo, procurement, and close. The product may be strong. The motion still depends on a pipeline of potential deals and the number of seats sold.

**Marketing-led** growth puts campaigns and content first. Useful. Not the same as PLG unless the content lands people inside a product that activates them.

Most companies mix motions later. Your job early is to name the **dominant** motion so product, channel, and model fit ([Brian Balfour's four fits](https://brianbalfour.com/essays/product-market-fit-isnt-enough)).

Two business metrics help compare motions. **Annual contract value (ACV)** is the average revenue from one contract for one year. **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** is sales and marketing spend divided by new paying customers.

| Motion | Who sells | First proof | First metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product-led | The product | User reaches value alone | Activation / time-to-value |
| Sales-led | Humans + process | Qualified opportunity | Pipeline, win rate, ACV |
| Marketing-led | Campaigns | Attention and intent | CAC, signup quality |

**Figma** let designers collaborate in the browser; teams adopted without a top-down design-suite purchase. **Calendly** proved value the first time someone booked a meeting without email tennis. **Workday** grew on sales-led enterprise HR/finance: long cycles, security review, implementation partners. Copying Figma's free-trial homepage onto a seven-figure ERP deal does not make you PLG. Hiring Workday's field motion for a free lesson site does not make you enterprise.

## Four fits (why PLG is a system)

Balfour's point: product-market fit is not enough. **Product, market, channel, and model** must fit each other.

| Fit | Question | PLG answer (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Does the beachhead get value fast? | Time-to-value measured in minutes or one session |
| Market | Will they adopt without a salesperson? | Self-serve buyers; low switching cost to try |
| Channel | How do they find you? | Product-led loops, organic, word of mouth, founder network |
| Model | How do you capture value? | Free forever, freemium, or free trial → paid |

A sales-led model with a PLG product (or the reverse) burns money. Enterprise ACV with no human sales and a 90-day security review is a fantasy. Free self-serve with a six-month onboarding call is not PLG either.

## Free forever vs free-to-paid

Both can be product-led. They are not the same business.

**Free forever:** the free surface *is* the product for that beachhead. Monetization may never come, or it comes from a different buyer, ads, services, or a later packaging bet. Education products and open tools often live here on purpose.

**Free-to-paid (freemium / trial):** free is the funnel. Paid is the business. You design the free experience so the user hits a natural limit or a desire for more depth, then convert.

Bush's work stresses: do not invent price before you know what free must prove. [Pricing as a business case](/learn/product/pricing-business-case) owns **willingness to pay (WTP)** and packaging. This lesson owns the **motion**: what free must do before money is honest.

| Pattern | Free must prove | Kill signal |
|---|---|---|
| Free forever | Beachhead returns and finishes the job | Vanity traffic; no completion; no return |
| Free-to-paid | Activation + a reason to pay | Signups without activation; paywall before value |
| Sales-led | Problem worth a meeting and a contract | Demos that never reach a champion |

## Activation is the first metric

Signup is not success. <Key>Activation is the first moment the beachhead gets the promised value.</Key>

Define it as a behavior, not a pageview:

- Figma: a second person edits the same file in the same session
- Calendly: a guest books a real meeting from the share link
- This site (specimen below): tech-lesson start or waitlist join from a non-friend; PM Lesson 1→2 as trust guardrail

Time-to-value matters. If activation takes a week of setup, your PLG motion is lying. Cut the path or change the promise.

Retention and expansion come after activation. [Product analytics and GTM](/learn/product/product-analytics-gtm) goes deeper on funnels and cohorts. Here: do not optimize acquisition until activation is real.

## The wedge

PLG needs a **wedge**: the smallest product experience that proves the job and invites the next step.

Not a catalog of features. Not a course platform. One path:

1. Land (URL, share, community)
2. Activate (first value)
3. Habit or hand-raise (return, waitlist, invite, upgrade)

**Canva** won creators with a free editor that shipped a finished design in one sitting; paid unlocked brand kits and team features later. The wedge was the finished artifact, not a suite tour.

### The loop after first value

A product-led loop connects four behaviors:

1. **Acquisition:** a target customer arrives through search, content, a shared artifact, or another channel.
2. **Activation:** that customer completes the job and receives first value.
3. **Referral:** the customer brings another target customer into the product.
4. **Retention:** either customer returns after a real opportunity to repeat the job.

For instance, follow a Canva-style collaboration loop. A creator arrives through a template search and exports a finished poster. That export is activation. The creator then invites a colleague to edit the design. The colleague opens the invitation, makes an edit, and exports the next version. The colleague has now activated through referral. If both return for another project next month, the product has retention evidence.

Count the behavior that completes each step:

- A template pageview shows acquisition volume, but an export shows value.
- An invitation sent shows use of the sharing control, but a referred colleague reaching first value shows referral quality.
- A notification-driven open shows a visit, but another completed design shows retained value.

Suppose the team tests an invitation prompt after export. The **primary metric** is referred colleagues who activate per activated creator. Invitation sends and acceptance rate diagnose where the loop changed. Spam reports, unfinished creator exports, and unwanted invitations protect trust as **guardrails**. [Experiments](/learn/product/experiments) defines those metric roles; [Product analytics and GTM](/learn/product/product-analytics-gtm) explains acquisition channels and retention cohorts.

If your wedge requires login, payment, and a certificate before value, you built sales-led friction into a PLG story.

## When PLG fails (and sales-led is honest)

PLG is wrong when:

- The buyer and user are different and the buyer will not self-serve
- Security, legal, or procurement must happen before any value
- ACV is high and implementation is custom
- The product cannot show value without human configuration

Then sales-led (or hybrid) is the fit. Do not shame it. Name it. Build product that helps sales win, not a fake free tier that never activates.

Hybrid is common later: product-led land, sales-assisted expand. Early stage: pick one dominant motion for the beachhead.

## Dual scoreboard (when demand is thin)

A pure PLG company treats near-zero demand as a kill or pivot signal. Some products have a **second scoreboard** on purpose: teach, portfolio, craft showcase, even when packaging waitlists are quiet.

That is allowed if you say it out loud. It is not fake PMF. Packaging and Stripe still wait on the product demand scoreboard. Lesson 3 already made that split for this site. PLG discipline means you do not confuse the personal scoreboard with traction.

## Specimen: this site

Strategy ([Lesson 3](/learn/product/product-strategy)): tech-for-PMs beachhead; free PM spine as trust and showcase; free tech as the wedge; waitlist before the tech curriculum; packaging only if demand earns it.

| PLG idea | This site |
|---|---|
| Motion | Product-led: public lessons sell the next step |
| Channel | Public URL + LinkedIn / PM communities / meetups |
| Wedge | Honest tech waitlist now; free tech lessons after signal |
| Activation (product scoreboard) | Non-friend waitlist joins; later tech-lesson starts and finishes |
| Trust guardrail | PM Lesson 1→2 completion |
| Free forever vs paid | Free lessons as the living product; paid depth only after funded hypothesis |
| Refuse | Login wall on free lessons; Stripe before demand; ads/SDR as the plan |
| Dual scoreboard | Keep teaching/showcase if demand is thin; do not call it PMF |

Sales-led would look like outbound to buy a cohort seat before anyone finished a lesson. That fights the product. Marketing-led alone would be posts without a path into Learn. The product is the motion.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What is happening |
|---|---|
| PLG = freemium checkbox | No activation definition |
| Optimize signup before activation | Acquisition theater |
| Paywall before value | Free-to-paid without the free proof |
| Call invitation volume referral growth | Recipients never reach value |
| Copy Figma-style self-serve onto high-ACV ERP | Model/channel misfit |
| SDR team on a free lesson site | Motion fights product |
| Call portfolio traffic PMF | Dual scoreboard confusion |
| Build certificates to "drive growth" | Course-platform sprawl |

## Before you go on

1. Name your **dominant motion** for the next two quarters: product-led, sales-led, or hybrid (and which part dominates land).
2. Write an **activation event** in one sentence (behavior + who + by when).
3. Draw the **wedge**: land → activate → next step (habit, waitlist, or pay).
4. Say whether free is **forever** or **to-paid**. If to-paid, what must free prove before price exists?
5. List one motion you refuse this quarter (ads, SDR, login wall, etc.) so the system stays coherent.
6. If sharing belongs to the job, define referral as the recipient behavior that proves value, not the invitation the existing customer sent.
